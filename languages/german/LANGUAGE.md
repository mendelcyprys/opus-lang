# German (Deutsch) — teaching notes

- **Folder:** `languages/german` · **TTS tag:** `de-DE` (`de-AT` / `de-CH` if Austrian/Swiss) · **Levels:** CEFR (Goethe-Zertifikat)
- **Standard:** standard German (Germany) unless the learner prefers Austrian/Swiss.

## Script & typing
- **ä ö ü ß** — long-press on the phone keyboard. ae/oe/ue/ss fallbacks are spelling mistakes → correct
  (ß after a long vowel/diphthong: *Straße, heißen*; ss after a short vowel: *Fluss, muss*).
- **Capitalize every noun** (and Sie/Ihr in formal address) — correct lower-case nouns.

## Notation in files
- Nouns: ALWAYS article + plural (*der Tisch, -e; die Frau, -en; das Haus, ¨-er*); n-declension nouns flagged.
- Verbs: Präteritum + Partizip II if irregular, separable prefix (*an|rufen*), haben/sein in Perfekt, case government (*helfen* + Dat., *warten auf* + Akk.).
- Prepositions: the case(s) they take.
- Vocabulary columns: German | English | Grammar info | Example sentence | First learned | Last reviewed | Mastery.

## Grammar map (A1 → B2)
- **A1:** present tense incl. stem change (du fährst), sein/haben, articles & gender, Nominativ/Akkusativ, V2 word order, questions,
  nicht vs kein, modal verbs, separable verbs, possessives.
- **A2:** Dativ (verbs & prepositions), two-way prepositions (in/an/auf + Akk./Dat.), Perfekt (haben/sein), subordinate clauses
  (weil, dass, wenn → verb-final), reflexive verbs, comparative/superlative, intro to adjective endings.
- **B1:** adjective declension (all three types), Präteritum, Genitiv, relative clauses, Konjunktiv II (würde, hätte, wäre, könnte),
  passive (werden), zu + infinitive, um…zu, als / wenn / ob.
- **B2:** Konjunktiv I (reported speech), passive with modals, Plusquamperfekt, n-declension, extended participle attributes,
  nominal style, verbs with fixed prepositions.

## Pitfalls
- Gender & plural must be learnt with every noun; case endings; verb-final in subordinate clauses; separable prefixes at the end;
  haben vs sein in Perfekt.
- False friends with English: *bekommen* (get), *Gift* (poison), *also* (so/therefore), *wer* (who), *will* (want).
- **Cross-language:** like Polish it has cases, but functions differ (Polish instrumental *z kolegą* ↔ *mit* + Dativ; no aspect — use adverbs/tenses).
